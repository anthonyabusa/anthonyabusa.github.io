Shipping a portfolio is easy; building one that actually reflects how you think is harder, and that is the part that matters.

Most portfolios become graveyards: a neat row of finished projects, frozen in place, while the real work moves on somewhere else. I did not want a headstone with better typography. I wanted a site that grows as I do, argues the way I argue, and leaves the person who lands here with something more useful than a curated shelf.

### The constraints I gave myself

**No templates.** If a stranger can deploy the same site in five minutes, it was never a personal brand. It was a theme wearing your name tag.

**Content before design.** A beautifully designed empty site is still empty. The thinking has to exist before it is worth dressing up; otherwise the design is just making the silence more expensive.

**Ship ugly, then sharpen.** The worst outcome is not a rough launch. It is a polished site that never ships, because every edge became an excuse to wait. I would rather build in the open and let the shape improve where people can actually see it.

### What you'll find here

Projects from consulting, product, and open source. Writing at the seam of business and technology, and in the places that feed it: philosophy, psychology, economics. Honest post-mortems on the work that did not pan out, because those are usually the ones with the most signal once the posturing gets stripped away.

So why build it in public? Because the alternative is building it in private, arranging the graveyard carefully, and hoping someone eventually wanders in.

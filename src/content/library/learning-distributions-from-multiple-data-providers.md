synopsis: "This study examines distribution learning from restricted conditional samples provided by multiple heterogeneous sources. The authors characterize learnability using a co-occurrence graph, establishing that connectivity ensures pointwise consistency while completeness enables PAC learning. The research further analyzes sample complexity, demonstrating that structural properties of the query family determine whether rates range from nearly linear to quadratic, with hierarchical structures facilitating efficient learning."
takeaways:
  - "Pointwise consistency is achievable if and only if the co-occurrence graph is connected on the target support."
  - "PAC learning requires the co-occurrence graph to be complete on the target support."
  - "Sample complexity for complete graphs ranges from nearly linear to quadratic depending on structural properties."
  - "Hierarchical comparability is a sufficient condition for achieving nearly linear sample complexity."
  - "Redundant overlap can lead to quadratic sample complexity by diluting informative signal."
  - "The full range of polynomial rates between linear and quadratic is attainable for different query families."
